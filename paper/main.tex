\begin{abstract}
Training data for visual world models can allocate a fixed budget to additional trajectory sources or to repeated sources under different physical conditions. We compare nominal state coverage, paired physics coverage, and an unpaired physics mixture using a fixed LeWorldModel implementation on OGBench Cube. Each policy uses 384 training-window records and 10,000 optimizer updates, with three training seeds and 64 source-episode-disjoint test groups. The held-out condition combines familiar mass and actuator-delay settings. Paired collection reduces raw free-running latent mean squared error by 3.45\% relative to the unpaired mixture; a bootstrap interval resampling training seeds and paired test episodes excludes zero. The unpaired mixture does not clearly improve over nominal state coverage. However, the paired advantage over the state baseline does not persist after coordinate standardization, and rollout cosine error is higher. A common train-only linear-decoder evaluation gives mean block-position errors of 14.87, 15.91, and 15.28 cm for state, paired, and unpaired policies, respectively, without an established paired-policy improvement. A source audit also distinguishes 384 window records from distinct dynamical sources. The results support a metric-dependent effect of collection policy on latent prediction, rather than a general advantage in physical dynamics or control. Because pairing changes source-state and action reuse, the comparison does not isolate pair correspondence at fixed unique-state coverage.
\end{abstract}
